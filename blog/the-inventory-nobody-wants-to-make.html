The Inventory Nobody Wants to Make

Jeff Crump · July 31, 2026

Before you spend a dollar on AI, spend two weeks on an inventory.

Every recurring task in your business that takes more than one hour of human time per week — write it down. What is it. Who does it. How long it takes. Which systems they touch. How often it goes wrong. That’s it.

Most SMB leaders won’t do this exercise, and their reasons are honest. It’s uncomfortable. You’ll discover work no one told you about. Some of it is duplicated across departments. Some of it exists because a system doesn’t do what it should. A little of it is invented by employees who quietly figured out a workaround years ago and never mentioned it.

That discomfort is exactly why the inventory is valuable. It’s a map of your operations that no one else in your business has, and until you have it, every AI investment you make is a guess.

Here’s the practical part: the inventory doesn’t need to be fancy. A shared spreadsheet with five columns is enough — task name, owner, hours per week, systems touched, and whether the person doing it would call it “high” or “low” judgment work. The low-judgment recurring work is where AI actually helps. High-judgment work usually isn’t. Most SMBs discover their high-value AI candidates in the first fifty rows.

I’ve watched clients avoid this exercise for a year and spend $40,000 on AI tools that landed nowhere. I’ve watched clients who did the inventory in two weeks find $50,000 of automatable work in their first pass.

The inventory is boring. That’s the point. Boring wins in this era, because everyone else is chasing the demo.

If your business hasn’t run this exercise, that’s where AI adoption starts. Not with a vendor call. Not with a strategy offsite. With a spreadsheet.
