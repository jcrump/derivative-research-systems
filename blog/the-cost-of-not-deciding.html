AI Adoption

The Cost of Not Deciding

Jeff Crump · November 20, 2026

The most expensive AI decision most SMBs make is not making one.

Here’s what “not deciding” actually looks like: employees are already using AI tools on their own accounts, on their own initiative, with your business’s data. Some of them are doing it well — genuinely accelerating their work and being careful with sensitive information. Some of them aren’t. You don’t know which is which, because you haven’t decided that this is something you’d have opinions about.

That’s a decision by default. It has costs, and the costs are compounding.

Shadow AI creates data security exposure. Your customer information is sitting in the memory of external AI providers, subject to their retention policies, potentially used in ways your legal team hasn’t reviewed. If a customer asks how you protect their data, you don’t have a good answer, because you don’t actually know where it’s going.

Shadow AI creates output inconsistency. Different employees are using different tools, producing outputs that reach customers with different quality standards. Some of that variance is invisible until a customer complains.

Shadow AI creates skill divergence. The employees using AI thoughtfully are gaining productivity advantages the rest of the team doesn’t have. That advantage compounds. Meanwhile, employees who never learned AI tools are becoming increasingly disadvantaged relative to their peers, and their managers don’t know.

Shadow AI creates competitive drift. Your business is either gaining an AI-enabled advantage or losing ground to competitors who are, and you can’t tell which because you never measured. A year of drift adds up.

The cost of all of this — measured in exposure, quality, and lost advantage — is much higher than the cost of deciding.

The decision doesn’t have to be complicated. It can literally be a one-page policy, three sanctioned tools, and a monthly office-hour where employees ask questions. Total cost: maybe two days of leadership time and $5,000 in tool subscriptions. That’s cheap insurance against the compounding costs of drift.

Here’s the honest part: most SMB leaders know they should decide but keep pushing it off, because there’s always something more urgent. The urgent things will still be urgent next month, and next quarter, and the year after that. AI is not going away. The decision is only going to get more expensive to delay.

Here’s a 90-day starter plan for the leaders who read this and decide today is the day:

Month 1: Publish a one-page AI usage policy. Communicate it. Answer questions.

Month 2: Sanction two or three specific AI tools your business will support. Provide access. Document use cases.

Month 3: Run one small, well-scoped AI pilot on a specific process. Measure it against a baseline.

At the end of 90 days you’ll have moved from drift to direction. That transition alone is worth more than most AI investments.
